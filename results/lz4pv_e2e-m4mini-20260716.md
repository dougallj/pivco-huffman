# lz4pv: LZ4HC parse + pivcoh v4 entropy, end-to-end

`extras/bench/bench_lz4pv.c`: the four-stream LZ4 split (lz4hc_split)
with literals + tokens + offset-high-bytes entropy-coded by pivcoh v4
(off-low + overflow raw; per-stream raw fallback), executed by the new
plane-aware split decoder (`lz4_split_decompress_planes` — offsets as
two independently codable byte planes, stride-specialized core, old
interleaved API preserved).  Lens wire built with
-DPIVCOH_LENS_WIRE_NO_MODE2 (decode-priority).  `PIVCOH_EFFORT` env
selects simple/balanced/faster/fastest; `LZ4_LEVEL` the parse (default
HC-9, matching the May analysis).  lz4 v1.10.0 clone at ext/lz4
(untracked) supplies lz4hc.h/lz4.c + the reference decoder.

**Correction (same day):** the first capture of this bench lacked the
house >= 100 ms DVFS warm loop; the warmed rerun (below — all numbers
in this file are post-warmup) moved pv-vs-LZ4 decode by ~1-5 pp.
The headline that changed: silesia decode is **parity-to-slightly-
under** LZ4 (−1..−5%), not "parity-to-faster"; the datasets advantage
shrank from +8..14% to **+6..11%**.  Ratios were never affected.
Run-to-run wobble on the mini is ~±3% on absolute MB/s; the pv-vs-lz4
delta within one run is the stable number.

## LZ4_LEVEL sweep at FASTEST (silesia geomeans)

Encode is ~97% parse, so the effort tier is free — FASTEST costs
nothing in ratio (3.190 vs balanced 3.187 at HC-9) and nothing in
encode.  The parse level is the real encode knob:

| parse  | ratio lz4 → pv  | enc MB/s | dec MB/s pv vs lz4 |
|--------|-----------------|----------|--------------------|
| HC-1   | 2.330 → 2.826   | **415**  | 5521 vs 5462       |
| HC-4   | 2.636 → 3.122   | 128      | 5895 vs 5873       |
| HC-6   | 2.704 → 3.173   |  89      | 6028 vs 6010       |
| HC-9   | 2.729 → 3.190   |  57      | 6067 vs 6068       |
| HC-12  | 2.757 → 3.219   |  25      | 6154 vs 6099       |

(HC-1 resolves to lz4's LZ4MID fast-HC strategy in v1.10.)  HC-1 is
the encode-competitive point: 7x the encode of HC-9 for 11% ratio give-
back, decode unchanged.  Note pv decode barely moves across parse
levels — entropy-coded streams shrink with better parses but execution
dominates.

## Like-for-like vs zstd-pivcoh L1 (same 12 silesia files, per-file geomean)

zstd-pivcoh = our pivcoh-literals zstd build (ALL_BALANCED knob),
`zstd -b1` per file; lz4pv at FASTEST, clean serialized runs:

| codec                 | ratio | enc MB/s | dec MB/s |
|-----------------------|-------|----------|----------|
| zstd-pivcoh L1        | 2.893 | 1457     | 2824     |
| lz4pv HC-1            | 2.826 |  398     | 5278     |
| lz4pv HC-4            | 3.122 |  128     | 5895     |
| lz4pv HC-9            | 3.190 |   57     | 5891     |
| plain LZ4HC-9         | 2.729 |    —     | 5958     |

* At matched ratio (HC-1, −2.3% vs zstd L1): lz4pv decodes **1.9x**
  faster than the already-pivcoh-accelerated zstd, but zstd L1 encodes
  **3.7x** faster (its double-fast parse vs any HC parse — the parse,
  not entropy, is lz4pv's whole encode bill).
* One HC step up (HC-4) beats zstd L1 ratio by +8% at 2.1x its decode,
  encode 128 MB/s.
* An encode-competitive lz4pv (~1.5 GB/s) would need a fast-parse
  splitter (LZ4_compress_fast emitting split streams), not an HC level.

## Positioning (same mini, same corpus)

* zstd-3 ratio at LZ4 speed: lz4pv balanced = **3.187 ratio at
  5.9 GB/s decode** vs zstd-3 3.200 at 1.79 GB/s (vanilla) / 3.191 at
  2.01 GB/s (our pivcoh-zstd) — ~3x the decode of even the improved
  zstd at equal ratio, within −1..−5% of plain LZ4 decode while
  compressing 17-24% denser.
* The May pivco is beaten easily: its literals-stream decode was
  4510 MB/s on cat-wiki (old scalar pivcoh); v4 balanced runs the
  same stream at ~8-15 GB/s (2-3x), silesia lits geomean 11.4 GB/s.
* Effort tiers barely move e2e decode (lits entropy is a modest share
  once execution dominates) but shaped trees lift the lits stream
  ~25% over simple; with the joint solve amortized into a parse-bound
  encode, FASTEST is the sensible default.
* Per-file (post-warmup, HC-1 fastest): text-heavy files still decode
  FASTER than plain LZ4 (dickens +33%, pride +38% at HC-9 balanced,
  reymont +20%, webster +10%) — the plane-split execution beats LZ4's
  interleaved parse and the input is smaller.  Barely-compressible
  files (sao −20%, x-ray −29%, mozilla −6%) pay entropy passes for
  little ratio, and hyper-compressible log_apache (−12%) has too few
  literals to win back its stream parses; a per-stream "skip coding
  when gain < threshold" knob would recover most of that.

Raw column meanings in the bench: enc = parse+entropy e2e; dec = full
container decode (entropy + execution); litMB/s = literals stream
entropy decode alone (the May-comparable number).
